Spurious correlations (Clever Hans)
17Shuo Yang | Technical University of Munich
Spurious correlations refer to the presence of seemingly meaningful relationships between 
variables that are, in fact, unrelated or coincidental. 
In machine learning, these correlations can arise due to:
• biases in the training data, 
• overfitting,
• other factors that lead the model to rely on irrelevant features for making predictions.

===== PAGE 45 =====
André Martinet (1908–1999) postulates a double articulation in any linguistic utterance.
• The first articulation consists in the articulation of
meaningful units with each other. A minimally meaningful unit is called a moneme.
The set of monemes is decomposed into lexemes (e.g., table, walk, etc.) and morphemes (e.g., -s, -ed, 
-ful, -ly, un-, etc.).
• The second articulation is the articulation of meaningless units with each other. A minimal phonological 
unit is called a phoneme.
Examples in English are: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, etc.

Inverse index: from words to docs
83Shuo Yang | Technical University of Munich
The inverse document frequency (idf) (Spärck Jones, 1972) of a document is 
a measure of the significance of a term in a document.
In tf · idf, its effect is to give more weight to less frequent terms. Less frequent 
terms are more representative of a document, and hence represent the 
document better.
Very frequent terms will be “neutralized” by inverse document frequency.
